Marram grass

A model of marram grass (Calamagrostis arenaria, formerly Ammophila arenaria) in one grid cell. Its state is a MarramCell:

  • shoot density and canopy height;
  • root and rhizome mass, and the thickness of the rooted sand layer;
  • root-zone soil moisture;
  • soil-biota pressure;
  • the current spells of submergence and drought.

Each process is a scalar function of the MarramTraits, with its source and evidence grade in its docstring. marram_rates collects every multiplier and mortality, marram_step advances a cell, and simulate_marram runs one cell under prescribed burial, water and flooding.

marram_experiment re-runs the published experiments to which the traits were calibrated, next to the published numbers.

On the grid, veg_model = marram (with process_vegetation) makes every cell a marram cell; the default veg_model = legacy is AeoLiS's vegetation. The grid model is set up by Aeolis.initialize_marram!:

  • each time step, Aeolis.marram_cover! gives the shear reduction its cover;
  • every veg_interval, Aeolis.marram_update! advances the cells and Aeolis.marram_colonise! colonises bare ones;
  • with process_dune_erosion, roots resist the cut-back of Aeolis.dune_erosion!: sand in a cell's rooted layer costs 1 + veg_cr / tau0 times as much of the waves' work as bare sand.

The grid model's parameters are:

ParameterDefaultMeaning
veg_modellegacymarram for this model
veg_filenoneinitial shoot density, as a fraction of N_max
veg_traits_filenoneTOML file of traits (see marram_traits)
veg_water_filenonetime (s), rainfall and potential evapotranspiration (mm per day); without it, roots never lack water

gamma_vegshear, vegshear_type, veg_sigma and veg_min_elevation act as for the legacy model. The veg_* state variables hold the cells and their diagnostics, and can be saved like any other.

Aeolis.MarramTraits — Type
MarramTraits(; kwargs...)

The traits of marram grass (Calamagrostis arenaria): growing season, burial response, shoot growth and mortality, canopy height, root-zone water, flooding, soil-biota pressure, lateral spread, roots and reinforcement, and drag. Every keyword defaults to the value in marram_info, which also gives its unit, evidence grade (:M measured on marram, :D derived from measurements, :P a prior), plausible range and source. marram_traits reads traits from a TOML file.

An unknown keyword, or thresholds of soil moisture or season that are out of order, raise an ArgumentError.

Examples

tr = MarramTraits(; burial_opt=0.35)
tr.N_max        # 470.0

Aeolis.MarramCell — Type
MarramCell(; N=0, h=0, Br=0, zr=0.3, s=0.16, P=0, Dsub=0, tdry=0, zdot=0)

The state of marram grass in one cell:

  • shoot density N (m⁻²);
  • canopy height above the bed h (m);
  • root and rhizome dry mass Br (kg m⁻²);
  • rooted sand layer zr (m);
  • root-zone relative moisture s;
  • soil-biota pressure P (0-1);
  • current spells of submergence Dsub and of moisture below wilting tdry (days);
  • trailing mean rate of bed-level change zdot (m per day).

Aeolis.marram_rates — Function
marram_rates(cell, forcing, tr)

Every multiplier and rate of the marram model for cell (a MarramCell) under forcing. The forcing is a named tuple with the day of year doy, the rate of bed-level change dzb (m per day), and optionally the wind speed u (m s⁻¹).

Returns a named tuple:

  • phi: the season weight (season);
  • d: the burial rate from the trailing mean cell.zdot (m per growing season);
  • Gb, fW, V: the burial, water and vigour multipliers;
  • growth: the potential relative growth rate r phi fW Gb V (per day), with the shoot density approaching V N_max;
  • m0, m_sm, m_dr, m_in: background, smothering, drought and flooding mortality (per day), and mortality, their sum;
  • elong: shoot elongation (m per day);
  • P_eq, Br_eq: the targets of pressure and roots;
  • cr, lam: root reinforcement (kPa) and frontal area index.

Aeolis.marram_step — Function
marram_step(cell, forcing, tr, dt)

The MarramCell after dt days under forcing, a named tuple with:

  • doy, the day of year;
  • dzb, the rate of bed-level change (m per day);
  • optionally rain and pet (mm per day), which update the root-zone bucket;
  • optionally s, a prescribed root-zone moisture, used instead of the bucket;
  • optionally submerged, true while the bed is under the sea, or the fraction of the step it was under water. Submergence accumulates while it lasts and resets after a step without any; repeated tidal flooding therefore counts as exposure to seawater (evidence P);
  • optionally u, the wind speed (m s⁻¹);
  • optionally supply, a capillary supply from groundwater (mm per day).

Without rain and s, moisture keeps its value.

The order of the update:

  1. The trailing mean of bed-level change.
  2. The bucket, then the spells of drought and submergence.
  3. The shoot density. Its exact logistic solution has the mortality of the step, where the flooding and drought losses are the exact integrals of their hazards over the step.
  4. Canopy height, roots (growing quickly toward their target and dying back slowly above it), the rooted layer and pressure (pressure_step; in a bare cell, pressure fades with tau_relief).

Every relaxation uses its exact solution, so states stay non-negative and bounded for any dt. A cell whose rooted layer is eroded away, or whose density falls below N_min, becomes bare.

source
Aeolis.simulate_marram — Function
simulate_marram(cell, forcing, tr; days, start=Dates.Date(2020, 1, 1), dt=1.0)

Run one cell for days days from the date start, with steps of dt days.

  • forcing(t, date) returns the forcing of marram_step at day t (from 0) and date date, without doy; the day of year at the middle of the step is added.
  • cell is a MarramCell and tr the MarramTraits.

Returns a named tuple of vectors: the time t (days), the date, every state variable, and the derived quantities and multipliers of marram_rates (phi, d, Gb, fW, V, growth, mortality, cr and lam). The first entry is the initial state.

Examples

using Dates
tr = MarramTraits()
sol = simulate_marram(MarramCell(; N=5.0, h=0.5), (t, date) -> (; dzb=1.4e-3), tr;
                      days=365, start=Date(2016, 6, 24))
sol.N[end]

Aeolis.marram_experiment — Function
marram_experiment(name, tr=MarramTraits())

Re-run a published experiment on marram grass with the single-cell model, and return the model's results next to the published ones. The experiments are:

  • :reijers2021: the one-year mesocosm of Reijers et al. (2021). Four tussocks (32 shoots) in 6.25 m² of fresh sand were buried by 0, 2 or 4 cm every fortnight from late June 2016, that is 0, 0.26 or 0.52 m per growing season. Compares shoots per mesocosm, the root:shoot ratio and shoot elongation.

  • :nolet2018: the growth response to burial over the 2015 growing season of a foredune planted in 2011 (Nolet et al. (2018)). Compares the model's response of a four-year-old stand, normalised to its peak, with the fitted quantile curves of NDVI and cover.

  • :dixon2004: 28 days without water, then rewatering (Dixon et al. (2004)). Root-zone moisture is prescribed to be below wilting. Compares survival.

  • :aptekar2000: survival after 1, 3, 7 and 13 days of continuous submergence in seawater, against the bud viability of rhizomes relative to the controls (Aptekar and Rejmánek (2000)).

  • :lammers2024: four weeks of a hot July without rain, under open-sided tents, on transplants in an embryo dune (Lammers et al. (2024)). Compares shoot growth with the ambient treatment (12.8 mm of rain) for a sparse planting of 10 shoots m⁻² (an assumption); the paper found no difference.

  • :homberger2024: the 132-day pot experiment of Homberger et al. (2024), with five marram shoots per pot of 0.081 m². Watering from 6.5 to 24.4 mm per week raised mean moisture by 2.2 % by volume per step. Plants died in pots with a mean of 3.3-4.8 %, and biomass rose linearly with moisture.

    The model prescribes root-zone moisture at seven levels from 4 % (an assumption within the range where plants died) in steps of 2.2 %. Pot hydrology is not modelled: the pots held water above the field capacity of free-draining sand. Compares shoot growth with the observed death in the driest pots and the continued rise with moisture.

  • :root_erosion: the reduction of wave-driven dune erosion by roots, c_r / (tau0 + c_r) (see dune_erosion!). It is computed in two ways:

    • with the root reinforcement of about 1 kPa that Figlus et al. (2014) measured in shear tests of their rooted flume dunes, where erosion fell by 33 % under regular and 8 % under irregular waves;
    • with the reinforcement of a model stand grown for five years under 0.3 m of burial per season, against the 36 % of intact cores of mature Ammophila under swash (De Battisti and Griffin (2020)) and up to 31 % in scaled flume experiments with marram (Kosmalla et al. (2026)).

Returns a named tuple with name, description, and model and observed values whose fields depend on the experiment.

Aeolis.marram_update! — Function
marram_update!(s, m, t)

Advance marram grass after the time step that started at t. The time step, accelerated by accfac, is added to the time since the last update, and to the time under water of cells whose water level is above the bed. Once veg_interval has passed, every cell is advanced by marram_step, with:

  • the mean rate of bed-level change since the last update;
  • the day of year at the middle of the interval, from refdate and accelerated time;
  • rainfall and potential evapotranspiration from veg_water_file, at the same time;
  • the fraction of the interval under water;
  • the current wind speed.

With groundwater, a root zone whose base is within 1 m of the water table is kept at field capacity, the capillary supply of Homberger et al. (2024). Below veg_min_elevation, plants are removed, as in AeoLiS. Bare cells are then colonised (marram_colonise!).

source
Aeolis.marram_colonise! — Function
marram_colonise!(s, m, phi, days)

Colonisation of bare cells over days days with season weight phi. A bare cell accumulates the hazard phi (v_front / L_eff) sum(w o_nb) / dx per day, plus establishment per year:

  • L_eff = L_s - season_ramp is the number of full growing days in a year;
  • o_nb = min(N_nb / (N_new N_max), 1) is the occupancy of a neighbour. Rhizomes extend from any established clone, however sparse, so a front advances at about v_front whatever the grid spacing;
  • the sum runs over its neighbours along each axis (only along x on a transect), with weight 1 - f_jump, and over the cells jump_distance (in m) away, with weight f_jump.

When the accumulated hazard reaches the cell's threshold (exponential, or 1 when veg_random is off), the cell is colonised with N_new N_max shoots, a canopy of h_new and a rooted layer of zr_new. The front speed v_front is that of patches on Ameland (Keijsers et al. (2015)), and the jumps those of Bonte et al. (2021).

Cells below veg_min_elevation, and cells that were under water for more than half the interval, are not colonised.

Aeolis.season_length — Function
season_length(tr)

The length of the growing season in days, season_end - season_start + 1 (183 for 1 April to 30 September). Burial rates are converted to m per growing season with it, the unit of Nolet et al. (2018).

source
Aeolis.season — Function
season(doy, tr)

The growing-season weight φ at day of year doy (fractional). It is 0 outside season_start-season_end, rises linearly over season_ramp days at the start, falls over season_ramp days at the end, and is 1 in between. Growth and lateral spread are multiplied by it; mortality and burial continue all year.

The window follows the NDVI time course of Nolet et al. (2018) and the April-August growth of Bonte et al. (2021) (evidence M); the ramps are a prior (P).

source
Aeolis.seasonal_burial — Function
seasonal_burial(zdot, tr)

The burial rate d in m per growing season of a rate of bed-level change zdot (m per day): zdot * season_length(tr). Negative for erosion.

source
Aeolis.burial_response — Function
burial_response(d, tr)

The growth multiplier G_b of burial d (m per growing season): a split Gaussian exp(-(d - b)^2 / (2c^2)). The optimum is b = burial_opt; the width is c = burial_c_lo below the optimum and burial_c_hi above it.

The optimum is that of Nolet et al. (2018) (evidence M, one site and one season). The widths are fitted to the one-year mesocosm of Reijers et al. (2021) (evidence D). It had as many shoots without burial as at 0.26 m per season, but a third as many at 0.52 m, and a symmetric curve cannot do both.

source
Aeolis.elongation — Function
elongation(d, tr)

The shoot elongation rate (m per day) at burial d (m per growing season): e0 + e1 * max(d, 0), a fit to the 0.3, 0.5 and 0.9 cm per day of Reijers et al. (2021) at 0, 0.26 and 0.52 m per season (evidence D).

source
Aeolis.smother_hazard — Function
smother_hazard(h, tr)

The mortality (per day) of shoots whose exposed canopy h is below h_min: max(1 - h / h_min, 0) / tau_sm, rising from 0 at h_min to 1 / tau_sm when the canopy is covered. Burial kills by covering the leaves: a single burial of about the canopy height is lethal, and 0.4 m leaves most plants alive (Maun and Lapierre (1984); Gilbert and Ripley (2010), A. breviligulata).

tau_sm is fitted to the mesocosm of Reijers et al. (2021) (evidence D). There, 4 cm of sand on the plants every fortnight, through a winter without elongation, left a third as many shoots, which the authors attribute to buried leaves. h_min is a prior (P).

source
Aeolis.water_stress — Function
water_stress(s, tr)

The water-stress multiplier f_W = 1 - ζ at root-zone relative moisture s, with ζ = ((s_star - s) / (s_star - s_w))^q_stress between wilting s_w and stomatal closure s_star. f_W = 1 above s_star and 0 below s_w. These are the form and values of Homberger et al. (2026) (evidence M).

source
Aeolis.evapotranspiration — Function
evapotranspiration(s, pet, cover, tr)

The loss of root-zone water to evapotranspiration (mm per day) at relative moisture s.

This is the piecewise function of Laio et al. (2001), as used by Homberger et al. (2026): 0 below the hygroscopic point s_h, rising to E_w at wilting s_w, then linearly to the unstressed rate at s_star.

The unstressed rate is E_w plus the share cover of min(E_max, pet) - E_w, where cover (0-1) is the shoot density relative to N_max. Transpiration per unit ground area scales with leaf area, while evaporation from bare sand stays near E_w, because a dry surface layer insulates the root zone. This is why the transplants of Lammers et al. (2024) were unaffected when the top 10 cm dried out (evidence D).

source
Aeolis.drainage — Function
drainage(s, tr)

Deep drainage from the root zone (mm per day) above field capacity: K_s (exp(b (s - s_fc)) - 1) / (exp(b (1 - s_fc)) - 1) (Laio et al. (2001)), with the values of Homberger et al. (2026). Their text and table disagree on b_leak (12.1 or 4.05).

source
Aeolis.bucket_step — Function
bucket_step(s, rain, pet, cover, dt, tr; supply=0.0)

Root-zone relative moisture after dt days of rainfall rain and potential evapotranspiration pet (mm per day), with canopy share cover. The balance is n Z_r ds/dt = I - E(s) - L(s), where I is rainfall less interception, plus any capillary supply (mm per day).

Rain is added first, and anything above saturation runs off. Losses are then integrated with steps that change s by at most 0.002, so s stays between s_h and 1 for any dt.

source
Aeolis.drought_hazard — Function
drought_hazard(tdry, tr)

Drought mortality (per day) after a spell of tdry days with root-zone moisture below wilting: k_dry once the spell is longer than t_dry, and 0 before. In Dixon et al. (2004), marram kept its leaf water content for about three weeks without water, and 80 % of plants recovered after four. This hazard gives 81 % (evidence D).

source
Aeolis.flood_survival — Function
flood_survival(D, tr)

The share of plants surviving D days of continuous submergence in seawater, 1 / (1 + (D / D50)^k_flood). It is fitted to the bud viability of rhizomes of Aptekar and Rejmánek (2000), relative to their controls: 80 % after 7 days and 13 % after 13 (evidence D, rhizome fragments rather than whole plants).

Aeolis.pressure_step — Function
pressure_step(P, d, dt, tr)

Soil-biota pressure after dt days at burial d (m per growing season), using the exact solutions of its equations.

Below its target P_eq(d), pressure builds up logistically from the seed P_seed: dP/dt = (P + P_seed)(P_eq - P) / tau_P. Root-feeding nematodes colonise fresh sand within a month (van der Stoel et al. (2002)), but their populations take years to build up.

Above its target, fresh sand relieves pressure: dP/dt = (P_eq - P) / tau_relief. Regular burial keeps marram vigorous, and plants decline when sand stops arriving (de Rooij-van der Goes (1996)). tau_relief, a few months for roots to grow into new sand, is a prior (P).

The slow start of the build-up reconciles two observations:

  • one year on fresh sand without burial cost the mesocosm of Reijers et al. (2021) only 4 ± 2 % of its shoots;
  • four years after planting, the field response of Nolet et al. (2018) fell by half without burial.

tau_P is fitted to both (evidence D); P_seed is a prior (P).

source
Aeolis.pressure_target — Function
pressure_target(d, tr)

The soil-biota pressure toward which a stand relaxes at burial d (m per growing season): clamp(1 - d / d_f, 0, 1). Fresh sand lets marram escape its root-feeding nematodes and pathogens, and pressure builds where sand stops arriving (van der Putten et al. (1993); de Rooij-van der Goes (1996); van der Stoel et al. (2002)). d_f is fitted to the fall of the field response of Nolet et al. (2018) below the optimum (evidence P).

Aeolis.vigour — Function
vigour(P, tr)

The vigour V = 1 - p_max P under soil-biota pressure P (evidence P). It multiplies both the growth rate and the shoot density that the roots can sustain, V N_max. A degenerating stand therefore thins, rather than only growing more slowly, as marram does on stable dunes (van der Putten et al. (1993)).

Aeolis.root_shoot_ratio — Function
root_shoot_ratio(d, tr)

The root:shoot mass ratio toward which roots grow at burial d (m per growing season). It is linear through rs0, rs1 and rs2 at 0, rs_d1 and rs_d2, and constant outside. Buried marram puts more into roots: 0.5, 1.0 and 2.5 at 0, 0.26 and 0.52 m per season in Reijers et al. (2021) (evidence M).

Aeolis.reinforcement — Function
reinforcement(Br, zr, tr)

Root reinforcement of the rooted layer (kPa). This is the Wu-Waldron estimate k_wu T_r RAR, reduced by the fibre-bundle factor k_fb (Wu et al. (1979); Schwarz et al. (2013)), and capped at c_max.

The root area ratio RAR is the volume of fine roots per unit area, spread over the rooted layer: f_fine Br / (rho_root max(zr, zr_min_cr)), with the tissue density of Walker and Zinnert (2022). Measured reinforcement in dune sand is 0.5-5 kPa (Figlus et al. (2014)). The tensile strength T_r of marram roots has not been measured (evidence P).

Aeolis.effective_cover — Function
effective_cover(lam, tr, gamma_vegshear)

The vegetation cover rhoveg = min(m_beta lam / gamma_vegshear, 1). With it, AeoLiS's shear reduction 1 / sqrt(1 + gamma_vegshear rhoveg) equals the Raupach drag partition with the measured m beta of live grass (Walter et al. (2012)), without its (1 - m sigma lam) term and capped as by Durán and Moore (2013). The legacy model is the case where rhoveg is the cover.
